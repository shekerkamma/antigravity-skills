Executive takeaways

Three patterns worth testing first

  1. Control the transaction boundaryGovernance products become harder to remove when every autonomous action passes through them.
  2. Enter through a narrow integration wedgeTranslate one painful document or system change before attempting end-to-end transformation.
  3. Own the exception workflowHigh-value agents coordinate evidence, deterministic checks, and human approval—not just content generation.

Decision framework

Transparent scoring, explicit assumptions.

Each idea is scored from 1–5 across five consistent criteria. A high score earns a discovery conversation, not an investment decision.

How to use this portfolio

Choose a priority concept, interview the named buyer, test whether the painful job is urgent and funded, and prototype only the narrow wedge. Re-score after customer evidence replaces assumptions.

01
Confirm the costly exception

Ask for the last real failure, delay, manual escalation, or audit issue—not a hypothetical use case.

02
Locate budget and authority

Identify who owns the outcome, who controls system access, and who can approve a pilot.

03
Test the narrow wedge

Prototype one bounded artifact or transaction with explicit human approval and measurable acceptance criteria.

Important evidence boundary
